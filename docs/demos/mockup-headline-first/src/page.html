Three real answers from the answer test of 2 November, each drawn twice at the Deck's real size: as the AI wrote it, and with one short opening line that carries the point. Under each panel is the reply-ready popup you see over a game, which shows only the answer's first words.

What the feature is: the AI is asked to open every answer with one short sentence that stands alone and gives nothing away. Nothing new is drawn on screen; the words change. Left is today. Right is the same answer with a headline. The headlines here were written by hand from each answer's own later sentences, to show the idea; the real ones would be the AI's, measured on the answer test before anything lands.

What to weigh.
  • For it: the popup over a game becomes useful by itself (you may not need to open the panel), and the first thing in the panel is the thing to do.
  • Against it: it is one more instruction for the small AI on the Deck to follow, so it can slip; and an answer that already opens well gains nothing (the third example). In September, 2 of 10 answers already opened with a line that stands alone.
  • "In bold" is an extra the roadmap entry does not ask for. It needs the AI to mark its first line reliably, so it is a little more work and a little more risk.
